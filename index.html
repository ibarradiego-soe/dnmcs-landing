How Maya actually closes

A named customer is a green light. Once she can point at a person who will pay, she wants a date, an owner, and a sentence the board can repeat. Market risk she will take. People-risk she will rename as timing and park.

Do not bring a tour of options. Bring one recommendation, the cost of being wrong, and who is on the hook. Three equal paths and she will pick the fastest one — then you will spend a month unsaying it.

  • She hears extra research as delay unless it is attached to a customer name.
  • She will override a slow pair if the raise narrative needs a locked story.
  • She decides in the meeting. If you needed another night, say so before she starts.

Say this Monday“Maya, I need a no on the enterprise SKU this week. Recommendation: kill it. Cost of being wrong: two design-partner conversations. Owner: me. Date: Friday.”

When the room tightens

Under pressure she narrows. Three options become one. The whiteboard gets a date. That is how Northwind ships. It is also how Priya’s model and Jordan’s “the story isn’t true yet” get treated as friction instead of signal.

Watch for

She skips the person who needed another day. She treats silence as agreement. She takes the people-decision last, then makes it in one sitting.

What works

Name the people-risk in a sentence she can refuse. She will engage with a written cost. She will not engage with a vibe.

Say this Monday“We’re about to close because you’re uncomfortable, not because the customer is named. Give the room until Thursday. I’ll come back with one recommendation.”

How to put work in front of her

First sentence is the ask. Second is the recommendation. Third is the date. Do not lead with context. Do not send a long memo the night before a board meeting and expect a careful no.

She hears dissent after a direction is on the page. Put a straw direction down, then argue. If you wait to be invited, the meeting ends with a date and you become the person who “never said anything.”

  • Ask → recommendation → what we lose if we’re wrong → who owns the next step.
  • Name the one thing you need her to stop doing.
  • Do not ask her to “weigh in” without a recommendation. Do not soften people-risk into “alignment.”

Say this Monday“Ask: approve Jordan’s pause on the launch narrative. Recommendation: yes, until he can name two customers who would repeat it. I need you not to lock the date in Tuesday’s staff meeting.”

The moves that waste this seat

  • Do not surprise her with a people-problem in a group setting. She will close it to end the discomfort, and you will own the wreckage.
  • Do not treat her speed as a quirk. It is how she reduces risk she can see. If you need time, buy it with a customer name or a written cost of being wrong.
  • Do not send “thoughts?” She will reply with a date.
  • Do not let Sam and Priya build a model she never asked for, then drop it the morning of the raise. Bring the model while the bet is still a bet.
  • Do not assume she knows she is skipping you. Write the sentence.

The hard lineIf the risk is a person, say the person’s name. Maya will not infer it from your tone.

What this pair only does together

Use them for

The gift

Conviction plus a market that can repeat it. Neither person does that alone without drifting — Maya into a locked fiction, Jordan into an endless draft.

Pricing and positioning

Maya hears “I need another week on the story” as avoidance. Jordan hears “lock it Friday” as a sentence the company will have to unsay in six months. Neither is wrong. Neither has said the decision-rights sentence out loud.

  • Jordan brings market doubt.
  • Maya hears delay and sets a date.
  • Jordan complies in the room and reopens it in Slack.
  • The team learns that “decided” means “until Jordan writes again.”

This is not a chemistry problem. It is an unowned call.

Complement versus echo

A room that covers the missing move — or a room that repeats itself.

Dnmcs does not assign personality badges. It shows decision pace, appetite for conflict, evidence habit, and where control sits. That is enough to see whether the table covers itself.

Complement

Different paces. Different appetites for conflict. Different evidence habits. A split in who holds the close. One person dates the bet; another keeps the story honest; a third wants the model before the date. The tension is usable once you name it.

  • The close and the market-true check sit in different seats.
  • Someone will enter the fight; someone will hold the cost.
  • Delegation is real — control is not piled on one name.

Echo chamber

Four people who decide the same way: same pace, same avoidance, same need for more evidence, same grip on control. Fast rooms get faster. Slow rooms stall. Nobody covers the missing move, and the next hard call feels unanimous until it breaks.

  • Everyone closes — or everyone waits.
  • Dissent happens after the meeting, if at all.
  • The org chart has titles. The room has one decision style.

How it works

Invite the table. Then use what you already have in the room.

There is no daily product to log into. One sitting per person, then the working surfaces — opened before the next hard conversation, not browsed like a feed.

  1. 01

    Invite the people who already sit there

    Co-founders, the C-suite, the pair that keeps missing each other. You send the invites. This is for the team you already have — not a screen for candidates.

  2. 02

    Each person consents, then completes

    Nobody is assessed by surprise. After an explicit yes, they complete the assessment in private. Raw answers stay private. They are never part of a teammate’s view.

  3. 03

    The three surfaces appear

    Each person gets a Working With Me card. Strategic pairs get a report. The team gets a dynamics map. Later hires can be onboarded on the same plan.

  4. 04

    Open them before the next hard conversation

    Use the card, the pair, and the map before the hire, the raise, or the fight you have been postponing. Walk in already knowing how the room decides.

FAQ

The questions a careful CEO actually asks.

How long does this take?

Each person completes one assessment in a single sitting. There is nothing to do every day after that. The value is the Working With Me card, the pair reports, and the map — opened when the next hard conversation arrives.

Will my CEO see my raw answers?

No. Raw answers are never shared. The CEO — and anyone else — sees only the derived cards and reports you have previewed and authorized.

Is this MBTI?

No. Dnmcs does not assign you a type. It shows how this specific team decides under pressure: how you work, how a pair collides, and how the room is put together.

Do we need a coach?

No. The $999 team plan stands on its own. A 60-minute debrief is available for $499 if you want someone to walk the surfaces with you.

Who can see my card?

Only the teammates you authorize. You preview what will be shared. If the audience or the scope changes, you will be asked to consent again.

Is this used to hire or fire people?

No. Dnmcs is not a screening tool and not a performance score. It is for people who already sit at the table — or who have already been hired onto it.

What about people we hire later?

New members can be onboarded on the same annual plan. They consent, complete the assessment, and their card and reports update the map.

What language is the product in?

English only. Every customer-facing string is written in English.
